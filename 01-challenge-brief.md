# NASA Space Apps Challenge Brief

## Summary

Long-duration missions expose astronauts to space radiation, isolation and confinement, altered gravity, and a hostile closed environment. These can bring immune changes, bone loss, cardiovascular events, and behavioral health problems. On long missions, astronauts carry much of the responsibility for spotting these changes in themselves.

**Challenge:** Build a health monitoring software that gathers health indicators and enables astronauts to evaluate and act on the status of their health.

## Background

- Space missions are getting more ambitious. NASA's Artemis program recently accomplished a crewed lunar flyby, and NASA and others plan to send humans to Mars.
- Longer missions mean more exposure to the hostile space environment, with both physical and mental health risks.
- NASA's Human Research Program has mapped five hazards of human spaceflight:
  1. Space radiation
  2. Isolation and confinement
  3. Distance from Earth
  4. Gravity fields
  5. Hostile closed environments
- These hazards can contribute to immune system changes, bone density loss, cardiovascular events, inadequate nutrition, behavioral health conditions, and others.
- Astronauts must be able to assess their own health during a mission, to prevent and treat problems that arise.
- Useful indicators named in the brief: blood pressure, heart rate, blood sugar, body temperature, daily sleep hours, mood.

## Objectives

Create a **visual tool** that helps astronauts assess their own health during space missions and provides guidance on treatments and health improvements.

- Data can come from any input: keyboard text, mouse clicks, camera images. Any data is fine as long as it represents a health indicator (for example blood sugar in mg/dL or ultrasound images).
- Use NASA's Human Research Program resources and public resources from other space agencies to decide which indicators are useful and how they support a quick assessment.

Questions worth answering in the solution:

- Which measurements can be performed in space?
- Why is an exam needed?
- How do spaceflight hazards affect these health indicators?
- When might a result warrant attention from the crew or from flight surgeons on the ground?
- What actions can be taken to prevent a critical health condition?

UI design matters for presenting metrics and diagnosis clearly. Animations and interactivity are encouraged. Example from the brief: a human figure where each body part is clickable and shows its function and health status.

## Potential considerations (optional)

- Health insights for different environments: spacecraft, space station, rover, base on another planet or moon.
- Prompt astronauts to take measurements or complete actions as part of mission routine.
- Possible features:
  - Gamification to help astronauts engage with health tasks
  - Dynamic charts showing indicators over time
  - Color coding so status is readable at a glance
- Do not assume astronauts know everything about assessing health. Provide simple instructions and explanations about measurements, indicators, and risks.
